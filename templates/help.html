What is Chart Replay
Like watching a match replay — but for stocks
Chart Replay lets you go back in time and experience historical price movement as if it's happening live. Perfect for beginners learning to read charts.
Replay historical data
Pick any NSE stock, select a date range, and watch candles appear one by one — exactly like watching the market live. You see only the past, not the future.
Draw support & resistance
Pause anytime and draw horizontal lines, support levels, resistance levels, and trendlines directly on the chart. Lines stay while replay continues.
Study indicators
EMA, SMA, Bollinger Bands, VWAP, RSI, MACD — all updating in real time as each candle appears. Toggle any indicator on or off.
Paper trade during replay
When you see a setup you like, click + Trade to log a paper entry with stop loss and target. Watch what actually happened — WIN or LOSS.
How to use it
5 steps to your first replay session
Takes less than 2 minutes to get started.
1
Pick a stock
Type any NSE symbol in the Symbol box — RELIANCE, HDFCBANK, INFY, TATASTEEL. The app automatically fetches data from Yahoo Finance. No login needed.
Start with a stock you know — makes it easier to relate news events to price movements
2
Select date range and timeframe
Choose From and To dates. For swing strategy — use 1D (daily) timeframe and pick 6-12 months of data. For intraday practice — use 5min or 15min and pick 1-2 weeks.
Tip: Start with 1D timeframe and 6 months of data. Each candle = 1 trading day.
3
Click Load Data, then ▶ Play
Data loads from Yahoo Finance (usually 2-3 seconds). Press Play — candles appear one by one. Use the speed slider to go faster or slower. Press Space to pause anytime.
Default speed is 5× — adjust to your comfort. 0.5× is best for studying individual candles.
4
Pause and analyze when you see something
When you see an interesting candle or pattern — press Space to pause. Draw your support/resistance lines. Check RSI (below 30 = oversold) and MACD (crossover = momentum shift).
Look for: price near support + RSI below 40 + MACD about to cross — that's a potential buy setup
5
Place a paper trade and keep watching
Click + Trade to log your paper entry with stop loss and target. Then press Play again. Watch what actually happened. Did price hit your target or stop loss? Your win/loss is logged automatically.
Always set a stop loss before entering. If you can't identify a stop loss level — skip the trade.
Chart Types
5 ways to view price action
Switch between chart types using the dropdown. Each shows the same data differently.
Candlestick
Standard chart. Green = price went up. Red = price went down. Shows Open, High, Low, Close clearly.
Heikin Ashi
Smoothed candles. Easier to see trend direction. Green candles in a row = strong uptrend. Best for beginners.
Line
Just the closing price connected as a line. Removes noise. Good for seeing the big picture trend.
Area
Line chart with shaded area below. Visually shows how far price has moved from lows. Good for overview.
Bar
OHLC bars. Classic style used by professional traders. Each bar shows open (left tick) and close (right tick).
Indicators
What each indicator tells you
Toggle any indicator on or off. Start with EMA 20/50 and RSI — add others as you get comfortable.
EMA 20 (blue line)
20-day exponential moving average. Fast-moving trend line. Price above EMA 20 = short-term bullish. Best for swing entries.
EMA 50 (orange line)
50-day EMA. Slower trend line. When EMA 20 crosses above EMA 50 = golden cross (bullish). Price above both = strong uptrend.
EMA 200 (purple line)
Long-term trend. Price above EMA 200 = bull market. Many institutional traders only buy stocks above EMA 200.
Bollinger Bands 2σ (white)
Standard deviation bands. Price touching upper band = overbought. Price touching lower band = oversold. Narrow bands = big move coming.
Bollinger Bands 3σ (amber)
Wider bands for extreme moves only. Price touching 3σ band is very rare — when it happens, a reversal is highly likely.
VWAP (amber line)
Volume Weighted Average Price. Resets each day. Most important intraday indicator. Price above VWAP = buyers in control.
RSI (purple, separate panel)
0-100 oscillator. Below 30 = oversold (potential buy). Above 70 = overbought (potential sell). Most useful when combined with price levels.
MACD (separate panel)
Blue line crossing above red line = bullish momentum shift. Histogram bars show strength. Best used alongside RSI confirmation.
Candle Patterns
Auto-detected patterns
The app automatically detects and labels these patterns as they appear during replay. A small label shows above or below the candle.
Doji
Neutral
Open and close are almost equal. Market is indecisive. Often signals a reversal when appearing after a trend.
Hammer
Bullish
Small body at top, long lower wick. Buyers rejected lower prices. Strong reversal signal at support levels.
Hanging Man
Bearish
Same shape as Hammer but appears in uptrend. Warning that sellers are starting to push back.
Shooting Star
Bearish
Small body at bottom, long upper wick. Buyers pushed price up but sellers pushed it back down. Reversal at resistance.
Bullish Engulfing
Bullish
Large green candle completely covers previous red candle. Strong buying. Reliable reversal signal with volume.
Bearish Engulfing
Bearish
Large red candle covers previous green candle. Strong selling. Exit signal if you're holding a long position.
Inside Bar
Neutral
Current candle is completely inside previous candle's range. Market consolidating. Breakout of the range = next move.
Morning Star
Bullish
3-candle pattern: bearish, small doji, bullish. Classic bottom reversal. One of the most reliable patterns.
Strategy Guide
What to look for when practicing
Two simple setups to start with. Practice identifying these on replay before trying any complex strategy.
Swing Buy Setup
✓Price is above EMA 50 (uptrend intact)
✓Price pulls back to EMA 20 or support level
✓RSI between 35-50 (not overbought)
✓MACD line crossing above signal line
✓Volume above average on the entry candle
✓Stop loss: below last support or EMA 50
✓Target: 2.5× the risk amount
BTST Setup (Buy Today Sell Tomorrow)
✓Stock making higher closes for 3+ days
✓Today's candle closes near day's high
✓Volume expanding on each up day
✓RSI between 50-65 (momentum zone)
✓No big news event tomorrow
✓Entry: today's close price
✓Exit: always next morning's open

FAQ
Common questions
Where does the data come from?
Yahoo Finance — free, no login required, covers NSE India stocks going back 10+ years for daily data. Intraday data (1min, 5min) is available for the last 60 days. The app adds .NS suffix automatically for NSE stocks.
Are the paper trades real?
No — paper trades are completely virtual. No real money is involved. The purpose is learning and strategy testing only. Think of it as a flight simulator for trading.
Why does Heikin Ashi use real prices for trades?
Heikin Ashi candle prices are synthetic (calculated, not real market prices). You can't actually buy or sell at a Heikin Ashi price. So paper trade entries always use the real closing price regardless of which chart type you're viewing.
Why can't I get 1-second candle data?
Tick-by-tick data is not available on free APIs. The finest available from Yahoo Finance is 1-minute candles. This is actually fine for learning — even professional traders make decisions on 1-minute or 5-minute candles, not second-by-second data.
Why does intraday (1min, 5min, 15min, 1H) data fail for older dates?
Yahoo Finance only stores intraday data for the last 60 days. For any date older than 60 days you must use the 1D (daily) timeframe — that goes back 10+ years. The app will auto-correct your dates if you accidentally pick a range older than 60 days for intraday timeframes.
How do I find good stocks to practice with?
Start with Nifty 50 stocks — RELIANCE, HDFCBANK, INFY, TCS, ICICIBANK, HDFC, TATASTEEL, SUNPHARMA, MARUTI, BAJFINANCE. These are liquid, well-traded stocks with clean chart patterns. Avoid penny stocks and very low-volume stocks.
How long should I practice before real trading?
A good benchmark: complete at least 50 paper trades across different market conditions (uptrend, downtrend, sideways) and achieve a win rate above 55% with average win larger than average loss. That typically takes 3-6 months of consistent practice.

Recommended workflow

2. Chart replay second — does it make sense visually on real candles?
3. Paper trade live — does it hold up in current market conditions?
4. Live trade small — only after consistent paper trade results.
